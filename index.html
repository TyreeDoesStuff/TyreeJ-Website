Three Very Different Snakes

Snakes are probably one of the most interesting types of reptiles because of how different they can be from each other. Some use venom, some use their strength, and some spend a lot of their time in water. I picked the black mamba, pythons, and anacondas because they are three snakes that stand out to me the most.

Black Mamba

The black mamba is one of the snakes I find the most interesting. Something I didn't know before is that the name is kind of misleading. Black mambas aren't actually black on the outside. Their skin is normally more gray, brown, or olive colored. The dark color inside of their mouth is where the "black" part of the name comes from.

Location
Eastern and southern Africa
Type
Venomous
Diet
Small animals

How They Hunt

Unlike pythons and anacondas, a black mamba doesn't need to wrap around its prey. It uses venom instead. Black mambas have fangs toward the front of their mouth that are used to deliver venom when they bite their prey.

Where They Live

Black mambas are found in parts of eastern and southern Africa. They can spend time both on the ground and around trees while looking for prey and shelter.

Pythons

Pythons are completely different from the black mamba because they aren't venomous. Instead they rely on their size and strength. There are a lot of different python species, so they don't all look or act exactly the same.

Location
Africa, Asia, and Australia
Type
Nonvenomous constrictor
Diet
Birds, reptiles, and mammals

How They Hunt

Pythons usually don't need to chase something down. They are ambush hunters and wait for prey to get close. They grab the animal with their teeth and quickly wrap their body around it. Depending on the size of the python, they can eat anything from rodents and birds to much larger animals.

Not Every Python Is Huge

When I think of a python I usually picture a massive snake, but their size actually depends heavily on the species. Some are fairly small while others can become some of the longest snakes in the world. Pythons also live in a bunch of different environments including rainforests, grasslands, swamps, woodlands, and rocky areas.

Anacondas

Anacondas are probably the snake I would least want to randomly see while walking around. They are large constrictors from South America and are really well adapted to being in water. Their eyes and nostrils sit high on their head, which helps them see and breathe while most of their body is underwater.

Location
South America
Type
Nonvenomous constrictor
Habitat
Rivers, streams, and wetlands

Green Anaconda

The green anaconda is one of the largest snakes in the world. Females can become much larger than males and these snakes spend a lot of their time in slow-moving water. Their color also helps them blend in with the vegetation around them.

What They Eat

Anacondas are carnivores and their diet depends on their size. They can eat fish, birds, turtles, capybaras, caimans, and other animals. Like pythons, they don't use venom to take down their prey.
